In possession

Attacking corners — Routine 1

We're big and we're quick. Routine 1 uses both: a grouped aerial threat that's hard to pick up, and two fast players held back so a cleared ball doesn't catch us out.

Outswinger from the wide midfielder. Two CBs + a CM group tight at the far edge, then attack the swing. Two fast players stay back.

The routine

  1. Outswinger from whichever wide midfielder is on that side — the ball curls away from the keeper, toward our runners.
  2. Both centre backs and one CM start grouped at the far edge of the box. Stand close so their markers can't tell who's attacking what.
  3. On the taker's signal, the group breaks and attacks the ball together — timed to meet the swing, not standing and waiting.
  4. The two free players read it: one on the edge of the D for the cut-back or second ball, one gambling on a near-post flick.

Stay back — don't get countered

  • CF stays back — our quickest outlet if we win it and break.
  • One wide midfielder or CM stays back alongside him, to kill their counter or start ours.
Why grouping works: three big bodies in a tight cluster force their defenders to sort out markers late. Someone always peels off free.

Out of possession

Defending corners — zonal posts, man-mark threats

A mix. Two players hold the posts as zones so nothing sneaks in front or back. Everyone else picks up their biggest threats man-to-man — goalside and touch-tight. A spare CM attacks anything loose, and the RM holds the edge of our box to start the counter.

CMs zone the near and back posts, with a spare CM free to attack anything loose. CBs and a wide midfielder man-mark their aerial threats. RM holds the edge of our box; CF stays high as the outlet.

Jobs

  • Two CMs — zonal, one each post. Don't get dragged out. Attack anything in your zone and clear it long.
  • Spare CM — free man in the box. Not tied to a post or a marker; first to attack anything loose — second balls, knockdowns, anything the zone and markers don't deal with.
  • CBs + a wide midfielder — man-mark their danger men. Goalside, touch-tight, arm across. Go with your man wherever he goes.
  • Keeper — commands the six-yard box. If he calls it, everyone clears the path.
  • RM holds the edge of our box — nearest out-ball to head clear and launch the counter the moment we win it.
  • CF stays high as our out-ball — the moment we clear, he's the target.
First rule: attack the ball, don't watch it. Whoever's nearest wins the first contact and clears it high, wide and long.
