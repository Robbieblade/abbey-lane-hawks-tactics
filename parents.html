Match day

Match day basics

  • Home matches usually kick off at 11am at Holmesfield. If there's an early Sheffield United or Wednesday game, we'll try to move kickoff earlier.
  • Away games are often only confirmed to us on the Tuesday or Wednesday before. We'll share the location and time as early as we can, on Spond and in the group chat.
  • Please respond to availability requests as soon as you can — it's what lets us plan the team.
  • Arrive in kit. Grip socks are fine, but they must be the same colour as our kit socks. Shin pads must be worn.
  • Wrap up in the winter months. Skins should ideally be black. We're looking into team jackets; in the meantime we have dry robes for the subs that fit most.

Leadership

Captains

  • We'll have three captains for the first half of the season, rotating so there's a different one each week. This also lets us swap the armband easily in-game. The captains change for the second half of the season.
  • The captain wears the armband — this is a league rule.
  • A captain's duties: set a positive environment, lead by example on kit, arrival and effort, and encourage teammates.
  • Our expectation is that every player acts like a captain, whether they're wearing the armband or not.

Fair minutes

Game time

  • We have 14 players for 9 spaces. We play with high intensity, so fresh legs are key to how we succeed.
  • Most weeks, players will average around 40 minutes. The goalkeeper plays the full 60.
  • If everyone is fit and available, a couple of players each week will get around 30 minutes. We manage this carefully and share it round.
  • We keep a spreadsheet of minutes and appearances to keep things fair and help plan substitutions, while still reacting to what's happening in the game.
  • Fair game time and a balanced team on the pitch come first.
If you ever have a concern about game time, please message Tim or Rob directly a couple of days before a game, rather than on the day or straight after. It gives everyone time to reflect on whether it's a significant issue.

Development

Positions

We feel we know the players' positions well by now. But we're always keen to hear from them if there's a position they'd like to try, want more advice on, or aren't enjoying — and we can look at adapting that for future games.

Full time

After the match

We'll have a quick chat with the players at the end of each game before they leave. Parents are always welcome to come over and listen in, or we can have a quick debrief as the children get to their cars.

Celebrating the boys

Monthly awards

We won't do awards at the end of each match, and we won't do an end-of-season presentation. Instead, there are three awards every month:

Supporters' PlayerParents vote for the player who stood out that month.
Players' PlayerThe children vote for their standout teammate.
Coaches' PlayerChosen by Tim and Rob.

How it works

  • Voting is through an electronic form with space to say why.
  • Winners receive a medal to keep and a trophy for the month, presented at training or before a match, with a bit of a fuss and some photo opportunities.
Why we do it this way: regular celebration gives the boys a really positive experience, and it avoids the anxiety some children feel at end-of-season presentations — around who has and hasn't won, and going on stage in front of strangers.

Training

Training

  • We put a lot of effort into training. We plan a six-week block of focuses and choose drills around the numbers we have at each session.
  • Please let us know your child's availability as early as you can. Last-minute issues can't always be avoided, but knowing in advance really helps us plan.

How we coach

Our approach — process over outcome

  • We want to win, and the boys are competitive — that's a good thing. But the scoreline isn't something we can fully control, so it isn't the only way we judge a game.
  • There are lots of outcomes that matter. You can lose a game and still do many of these brilliantly:
EffortAttitudeDecision-makingLooking after the ball Encouraging teammatesEnergyInvolvementEnjoyment
  • So we focus on the process — because we can't just decide to win, but we can control the things that make winning, and playing well, more likely. Get the process right and the good outcomes tend to follow.

How the boys can affect the outcome

These are things every player has control over — and where you can really help at home.

Energy & involvement

Look after yourself before the game.

A good night's sleep and the right food and drink before a match means more energy to press, make runs and stay involved for their minutes. It starts the night before, not at kick-off.

Technique

Practise, don't just hope.

Want a better first touch, pass or finish? It comes from practising and working on the feedback we give — not from hoping it turns up on match day. Ten minutes in the garden adds up.

Decision-making

Listen and watch football.

Better decisions come from listening to advice, asking questions, and watching good players in their position — noticing why they do things, then trying it themselves.

Attitude

Handle the frustration.

Things won't always go their way. Learning to be disappointed without sulking or giving up — and getting straight onto the next job — keeps them, and the team, in the game.

  • None of these are about being the most talented player. They're choices any of the boys can make — and they're the same habits that help well beyond football.
How you can back this at home: help with the process — good sleep and food before games, a few minutes practising, watching football together — and praise effort, attitude and good decisions, not just goals and results.

Get behind them

Sideline support

Home or away, we'd love plenty of support from the sidelines. Positive encouragement helps the boys play with the freedom to try things, make their own decisions and enjoy their football. Get behind them and enjoy the season with us.
