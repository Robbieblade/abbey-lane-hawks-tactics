In possession

Our goal kicks — build through them

We play through their first press, not over it. Three central midfielders give us overloads. Move them until a lane opens, then go. Only go long when they've shut everything down.

Deep CM drops between the lines to make a 4v2. Wide midfielders stay wide and high, CF pins their centre backs.
Keep itPossession first. Don't gift them the ball.
Overload the middleThree CMs beat two pressers.
Then find spaceWide midfielders and CF once the press is beaten.

Passing priorities — GK scans first

1Nearest centre back

3Opposite centre back
4Switch across the back
5Wide midfielder in space

Rehearsed pattern: GK → CB → deep CM → advanced CM turns and drives → wide midfielder receives wide → CF spins into the opposite channel.
If they press with two forwards

Two CBs plus the deep CM against their two = a 4v2 with the keeper behind. Someone is always free.

  • Don't rush. Move them side to side until a lane opens.
  • Keeper stays involved as the spare man to switch play.
  • Deep CM keeps moving — angles, never straight lines.
If they mark man-to-man

Don't force a short pass into a marked player. Go to the rehearsed direct pattern:

  1. Keeper plays straight into the CF.
  2. CF protects it and lays it off.
  3. One advanced CM runs underneath to collect.
  4. Both wide midfielders immediately sprint beyond the CF.
  5. Second CM supports underneath for the loose ball.

Out of possession

Defending their goal kicks — win it high

When their keeper has it, we press together, force one side, and try to win the ball high enough to score within a few passes. Not a flat five — one CM starts deeper so we've got cover and someone attacking long balls forwards.

Starting shape: CF pushed high, midfield across, holding CM and both CBs deep in our half as cover.
Force mistakesMake them rush and hurry the touch.
Block the middleNothing through us — send it round the outside.
Win it highTake the ball close enough to score.

The two who hunt together

  • CF curves the run, forces one side, never presses alone.
  • Nearest CM sprints with him, stops the turn, forces it backwards.
  • Holding CM starts deeper, screens the middle, reads long balls.
  • Wide midfielders narrow first, then explode wide to force play down the line.
  • Centre backs stay connected and hold as cover.

If they play short to a centre back
  1. CF presses the receiving centre back.
  2. Nearest CM sprints and joins him.
  3. Second CM blocks the pass into their midfield.
  4. Holding CM stays deep, screening.
  5. Wide midfielders squeeze inside, ready to jump out.
  6. Whole team shifts across together.
We're not trying to tackle straight away. We force it backwards, sideways, or into a rushed clearance.
If they go long
  1. Holding CM — already underneath it — is first to attack the ball, moving forwards onto it.
  2. Both centre backs hold their positions behind as cover.
  3. Second CM collects the knock-down and loose ball.
  4. Wide midfielders squeeze inside.
  5. CF stays high, ready to go the moment we win it.
The dropped CM attacks it, not a CB — that wins the ball moving forwards and keeps both centre backs in position behind as insurance.
If they switch across the back
  1. Nobody chases on their own.
  2. CF curves his run to the new side.
  3. Nearest CM replaces him if he's dragged out.
  4. Whole midfield shifts across as one unit.
The ball should never travel through the middle. Make them go all the way round the outside.
If the keeper throws or drop-kicks
  1. CF positions to block the quickest throw into the middle.
  2. Nearest CM joins him.
  3. Holding CM drops.
  4. Wide midfielders tuck inside.
  5. Everyone stays compact until the ball leaves his hands, then reacts.
Don't press before he releases it — he'll just throw around you.
If they go long: the dropped CM attacks it first, both CBs hold behind as cover, second CM takes the knock-down.

Why the deep CM matters

Because he starts behind the play, he attacks long balls moving forwards instead of backpedalling. That wins more headers and keeps both centre backs where they should be — behind him, as insurance if the first ball is lost.

Hunt in pairs. Attack the ball. Win it high.
